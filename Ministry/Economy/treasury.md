# alexander treasury

let every coin arrive beneath the sun,
and leave by paths that any eye may trace.
no vow is made concerning what is done;
a supervillain spends it case by case.

the promise is not where the money goes.

the promise is that anyone may look.


## feasible now

yes.

the first version is almost boring.

affiliate networks pay in fiat.

keep that money in an account
used only for alexander activity.

record what arrived.

when money should join the treasury,
buy bitcoin and record the conversion.

if an expense can be paid in bitcoin,
pay it from the treasury.

if an expense requires fiat,
sell or withdraw what is needed
and record both sides of the movement.

the chain will show the bitcoin.

the ledger will explain the rest.


## what gets promised

no permanent allocation formula.

no claim that all profit must remain untouched.

no claim that the founder cannot be paid.

no claim that every decision will look wise in hindsight.

capital is allocated as appropriate
by a supervillain.

the accountability is simpler:

publish what came in,
what went out,
and enough description
for another mind to judge the act.


## the public ledger

at minimum, record:

revenue;

fees and refunds;

operating expenses;

compensation;

tax reserves;

fiat held;

bitcoin bought and sold;

treasury balance;

transaction ids where bitcoin moves.

this need not become accounting theatre.

a small treasury can publish a small ledger.

scale the ceremony with the money.


## the wallet

private keys stay private.

the treasury may begin
as one hardware-backed self-custody wallet.

publish the address or watch-only information
needed to verify the balance and transactions.

later, if one signer becomes too much trust,
move to multisig.

bitcoin already supports this well.


## code can guard the door

the more interesting future
is not merely putting money on a blockchain.

it is letting code constrain
how the money may leave.

native bitcoin can already express
useful spending policy:

multiple required signers;

absolute or relative timelocks;

recovery paths;

combinations of keys and time.

miniscript gives these policies
a structured language that software can analyze.

a treasury might one day say:

two of three stewards may spend normally;

or a recovery key may act
only after a long delay;

or reserves may remain locked
until a known date.

these are real forms
of executable governance.


## code cannot yet judge justice

bitcoin script is deliberately narrow.

it does not natively run
an arbitrary governance program
that reads the world
and decides whether an expense is righteous.

projects such as bitvm
aim to verify arbitrary computation
without changing bitcoin consensus rules.

that work is fascinating.

it is also still a frontier,
not the foundation
on which an early treasury should rest.

the sensible path is incremental:

first make custody visible.

then make approval distributed.

then make simple rules executable.

only then ask
which larger judgments deserve code.


## entity

an entity is useful
when it solves a problem worth its cost.

contracts,
employees,
outside capital,
shared ownership,
or meaningful liability
may force the question early.

revenue alone does not.

for example,
a california llc currently owes
an $800 annual tax
even when the business loses money.

so a tiny experiment
should not incorporate
merely to look serious.

the trigger should be revisited
as the economics become serious.

the companion file
`treasury_triggers.md`
sets those bells.


## compensation

compensation is an expense.

report it.

that is enough
for the first constitution.

the public may decide
whether the supervillain
has developed expensive taste.
