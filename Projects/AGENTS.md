# Project work

Projects turn ideas into things someone can use. Keep each project self-contained. Its `README.md` should state the current conclusion in a minute; its task list and evidence should let a skeptical reader inspect how we got there.

For research-heavy work:

1. **Plan in a diff.** State the decision to be made, competing hypotheses, research tasks, outputs, and a stopping rule. Make the plan reviewable before treating a concept as selected.
2. **Work in bounded tasks.** Give each task an owner or agent if useful, a question, a method, an evidence location, and a completion test. Separate independent searches; reconcile overlap before counting sources.
3. **Keep the trail.** Record search dates and queries, inclusion and exclusion rules, source URLs, observations, contradictions, and limitations. Distinguish a seller's claim, an individual anecdote, an inference, and measured behavior. Do not convert a quick scan into an exhaustive survey.
4. **Write upward.** Raw source records support task syntheses; task syntheses support a concise project brief. The brief links downward. A chat reply reports the decision, the depth actually reached, and what remains uncertain.
5. **Challenge the preferred answer.** Look for disconfirming evidence and neglected customer types. An agent playing a persona may expose assumptions; it does not stand in for an interview or a real customer.
6. **Use code where it earns its keep.** Scrapers, analysis, simulations, and tests must have inputs, assumptions, reproducible commands, and interpretable outputs. A simulation is a model to falsify, not proof that people will behave that way.
7. **Review at gates.** Separate discovery, concept selection, prototype, playtest, and commercial launch. Record what was decided and why. Update or retire claims when new evidence arrives.

Do not force a source count to look impressive. Breadth targets are checks against tunnel vision; source independence, coverage, and unanswered questions decide whether the work is ready.
