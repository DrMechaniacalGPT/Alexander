# Scene treatment: the house that changes the answer

Working episode title: **Best Robot Vacuum? The Tests That Change the Answer**. Target roughly three minutes, subject to a real voice read and editing. Time bands below are an editorial map, not rendered timing. The whole film must earn its runtime. No opening logo or host biography.

The creative device is one coherent house: first we see it at floor level, then understand its different demands. Two versions share this treatment: an original otter guide, or an unseen narrator and camera-led scenes. The named products appear as accurately sourced editorial assets, not generated replicas carrying invented performance.

## Rhythm and sound

These bands include roughly 355–400 spoken words, planned breathing space and evidence inserts. They are not a mandate to pad to three minutes. A 2:45 edit is fine if every decision lands. The host should appear only where expression or a question helps, with silent coverage under voiceover elsewhere. This also reduces dependence on perfect full-time lip-sync.

Sound belongs to the shot: room ambience, small tactile movement, a restrained transition into macro scale. Exact product noise requires a real recording under known conditions. Music should not run loudly through a comparison. Source labels stay on screen long enough to register. Never compress delivery simply because a generation ended at six seconds; cut to coverage and let the voice finish naturally.

## What must be real versus generated

| Material | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Home, fictional guide, macro carpet atmosphere | Generated, visibly an editorial world; no fabricated product result |
| Exact product name and appearance | Verified source/authorized image or controlled accurate asset |
| Pickup percentage, test method, category weight | Sourced and added in editing |
| Original reviewer footage | Only if use has been cleared; otherwise faithful original explanation and link |
| Maintenance mechanism | Exact-model demonstration or checked technical animation; do not guess with generative video |
| Character reaction or visual joke | Fiction, with no implication of personal product ownership/testing |

No shot in this plan requires forging a real review, a real reviewer, or a product test. The documentary voice comes from the quality of the comparisons, not from pretending to have a lab.
