# Packaging that matches this film

The thumbnail the owner liked remains the first asset to recover, not a design to replace by default. No CTR test, title score or guaranteed-virality claim is made here.

## Recommended promise

**Working title: Best Robot Vacuum? 3 Things the Winner Badge Misses**

It combines the established category search with the actual episode: carpet hair, obstacles and upkeep. The question mark matters less than delivering bounded buying guidance immediately. Do not imply we have ranked the whole 2026 market. The title should not promise to explain exactly why the hair labs disagree, because that cause remains unresolved.

Alternative: **Before You Buy a Robot Vacuum, Check These 3 Tradeoffs**. More precise promise, weaker exact “best robot vacuum” phrase. These are two hypotheses for the same content; no extra vidIQ scoring credits are needed to invent them.

Avoid “Every Review Is Wrong,” “We Tested Them All,” “The Best Robot for Pet Hair,” and giant source-count claims. They would misrepresent either the original reviewers, our hands-on work, our unresolved pet-hair recommendation or our depth of review.

## Thumbnail brief if a revision is needed

One room, a clear wood/rug boundary and one meaningful focal point. A small award beside the rug can carry the question; if the otter direction survives motion testing, its restrained glance can add personality. Keep the animal subordinate to the buying problem. Use at most one short phrase such as **BEST FOR WHO?** Source grids, publisher logos and multiple model names belong inside the video/companion, not in unreadable thumbnail detail. The first scene must pay off the exact visual promise.

The recovered liked thumbnail may already solve this better. Compare it at phone size with the chosen title before commissioning changes. Do not replace it just to match a newly invented mascot.

## Demand evidence and limits

The inherited [September 25 discovery snapshot](../../../DISCOVERY.md) records vidIQ estimates of roughly 47,568 monthly searches for “best robot vacuum,” 22,230 for pet hair and 11,863 for hardwood floors. These are inherited tool estimates, not independently rerun numbers or guaranteed reachable viewers. They justify investigating buyer-specific needs. They do not justify a pet-hair-winner title when the script cannot supply that answer.

No new vidIQ credits were consumed for this pitch. Reserve them for a targeted statistics/keyword refresh or actual competitor analysis when the content promise is settled; do not spend generation credits to produce generic assets.

## How we learn after a publishable film exists

First, have a small set of target buyers explain what they expect from each title/thumbnail and what they learned from the opening. This is qualitative screening, not a statistically valid CTR experiment. Then use an available platform experiment or carefully logged comparable exposure to assess packaging; do not call a before/after view increase causal evidence when impressions, audience or timing changed.

Judge usefulness alongside attention: can a viewer name the recommendation's conditions, locate an original test and avoid the wrong purchase inference? Short watch time alone can mean either failure or a satisfied answer-first viewer. Retention, source clicks and viewer feedback must be interpreted together.
