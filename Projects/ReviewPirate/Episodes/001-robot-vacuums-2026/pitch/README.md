# A film worth testing

**Recommendation: make a short, cinematic review inside one coherent house, with an original otter guide tested against a host-free version.** Start with useful buying guidance, then let the camera reveal why the answer changes from room to room. No final mascot or brand is chosen.

The rejected sample failed the brief: a rendered file was treated as a creative milestone without proving the visuals, voice or viewer experience. It is not the production baseline. The replacement is a directed scene with real motion, an adult performance and carefully edited evidence. A strong still image cannot prove any of those things.

## What you would see

We begin at the height of a robot vacuum. Warm light crosses an oak floor; a rug becomes a landscape of fibers. The first 25 seconds offer two bounded buying paths and a warning about embedded pet hair. We then move through three questions: **what needs cleaning, what could stop the robot, and what work remains for you?** The house makes the consequences visible. Precise results and source credits are added in editing; generated scenes never masquerade as product tests.

The otter adds a small, expressive guide with dry humor. The alternative uses the same cinematic home and narration without a presenter. I favor trying the otter because it could give the channel a recognizable presence. That is a creative hypothesis, not evidence that viewers prefer otters. If its performance feels childish or artificial, the host-free version should stand on its own.
