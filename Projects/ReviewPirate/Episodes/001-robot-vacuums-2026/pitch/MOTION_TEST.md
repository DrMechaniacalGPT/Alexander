# First motion test: a scene, not another full rough cut

Proposed experiment only. Free generation is authorized in principle, but no video generation or paid spend occurred in this research pass. A free Flow route was checked but requires Google sign-in in the current browser. Do not create an account, buy access or use vidIQ generation credits as a workaround.

## The excerpt

A 12–18-second capability excerpt, separate from the buyer-answer opening. One on-camera test sentence: “A great score can hide a weakness that matters to you.” Off-screen continuation: “Let’s look at what was actually tested.”

The line is an editorial bridge, not a substitute for the sourced explanation that follows. It deliberately avoids difficult product and publisher names during the performance test. The full episode must show which robot, which tests, and why unlike metrics cannot be averaged.

## Three shots

**M01 / 4–5 seconds / presence and camera.** Floor-height dolly along the boundary between oak and rug. An unbranded robot is parked, with no cleaning action. Shared coverage for both versions: camera discovers a visible strand among fibers. No walking character. Narration can start immediately. Warm room tone, no music swell demanding an emotional reaction.

**M02 / 5 seconds / performance.** Medium close view of the guide looking from the rug to the viewer and delivering “A great score can hide a weakness that matters to you.” One subtle glance, no broad gestures. Host-free alternative: macro fibers with a gentle focus pull; narrator delivers the identical line off-screen. Avoid multiple speakers or moving camera during the speech test.

**M03 / 4–5 seconds / continuity and evidence.** Return to the wide room from a second angle with the same geography, without a character; use this as shared coverage. Hold a clean area for two evidence cards added later. Off-screen continuation: “Let’s look at what was actually tested.” If this line needs more time, lengthen the edit; do not rush the voice to fit the nominal shot budget. A 15–18-second result is acceptable if that is the natural delivery.

## Model-independent prompt specifications

M01: “A slow floor-level dolly across an oak floor toward the edge of a cream textured rug in a warm, lived-in home. Muted teal doorway in the background. A generic dark round robot vacuum remains parked on the wood. Late afternoon window light from camera left. One continuous shot, restrained movement, realistic textures. No text, no cleaning, no disappearing debris, no change to the vacuum shape.” Add approved single-frame reference; add host only in the character variant.

M02 character: “The same adult river otter from the character reference, wearing the same faded olive chore jacket, already seated in a stable pose in the same room. Medium close-up at its eye level. It glances down at the rug, then looks naturally toward the camera. In a calm conversational adult voice it says: ‘A great score can hide a weakness that matters to you.’ Subtle mouth motion and a single restrained expression change. Stable camera, consistent eye size and fur, room ambience, no music, no extra words, no subtitles.” Native voice is a candidate, not a locked performance. If voice consistency fails across shots, move speech off-screen and audition a separate licensed voice.

M02 host-free: “Extreme macro photographic view among cream rug fibers with a fine dark strand of hair looped around them. Gentle lateral camera drift and subtle focus pull. Raking daylight, natural materials, physical scale remains stable. No cleaning action, no changing fiber geometry, no typography.” This visual is explicitly an illustration of a material, not a reproduction of a lab's exact protocol.

M03: “A wider second angle in the same room, with the same cream rug, teal doorway, sofa, window direction and parked generic vacuum. Camera gently pulls back. Preserve the room and subject. Leave clear negative space on the left for editor-added evidence. No objects transforming, no text.”

## One canonical experiment and cost allocation

**Test the same M02 character performance in Veo 3.1 Fast, then Gemini Omni Flash 1.1.** Veo has the nearest visually sampled reference for a furred character in a believable interior. That gallery does not prove the exact current Fast version or its voice quality. Omni has relevant controls and a lower iteration price, but price cannot establish artistic suitability. See the [selection recheck](research/OMNI_SELECTION_RECHECK.md).

**Free gate, at most 50 Flow credits:** one eight-second reference-based Veo Fast M02 at 20 credits; one six-second Omni 720p M02 at 10; reserve 20 for one Veo retry or two Omni retries after diagnosing a specific failure. Use identical individual references, words, framing and intended performance. Compare their common useful duration, not the extra generated seconds. One output per request; verify the actual quote. Reference/voice preparation is excluded and must reduce the available generation allowance if charged.

This first gate tests whether either route can produce a credible performance. It does not promise a complete cut, a host/no-host comparison or reliable model rankings. Native voices may differ and are part of the route assessment. A real listening review remains necessary. If neither succeeds, try host-free motion; do not preserve the mascot by accepting bad acting.

**Next, compare treatments within the accepted route.** Build the 12–18-second host and host-free edits using the same model, same accepted spoken audio, shared M01/M03 coverage, timing and grade. Extract the accepted M02 speech for voiceover in the host-free edit. This isolates the host’s contribution more usefully than comparing unrelated models, voices and cuts. Internal review is not an audience preference test.

**Paid experiment ceiling depends on the chosen route:**

- If Omni leads: nine six-second Omni generations (90) + three Veo comparators (60) + two optional targeted Omni edits (80) = **230 video credits**.
- If Veo leads: nine eight-second Veo generations (180) + three Omni comparators (30) + two optional targeted Omni edits (80) = **290 video credits**.

Count the initial model-gate attempts inside these allocations; do not generate a duplicate batch. The nine main-route attempts cover three host performances, three host-free shots, two shared coverage shots and one contingency. Reference/voice preparation is separately quoted. Spend reserves only to resolve a named problem. A 40-credit edit is justified only for a near-keeper worth preserving; compare with the cost of a fresh generation.

Both scenarios fit the documented 1,000 monthly credits in Google AI Pro, displayed at $19.99/month before tax. This remains a proposal, not a purchase. Account eligibility and exact quotes must be checked. Higgsfield Plus at $59 is a fallback for a specific unresolved failure, not an automatic second subscription.

Log model/version, prompt, references, duration, resolution, audio, output count, quote, attempt, rejection reason, accepted seconds and editing effort. After three materially different failures on the same shot, diagnose or change route rather than repeat nearly identical prompts. Never rush a voice line to fit a six-second generation; cut to coverage and let it finish naturally.

After the scene language works, test a **second 12–20-second excerpt from the evidence-heavy middle**. It must combine one real result, readable source credit and a cinematic transition. A gorgeous opening cannot conceal a middle that falls back to slides.

## Review record

| Criterion | Evidence to record | Reject condition |
|---|---|---|
| Motion | Full normal-speed viewing notes | Sliding limbs, shape drift, accidental object motion or a camera move that distracts |
| Character | Side-by-side shot comparison and normal-speed continuity | Eye/jacket/scale changes, babyish performance, mismatched voice |
| Audio | Actual listening review on speakers/headphones | Wrong words, stiff cadence, noise, name errors, unexplained voice change |
| Editorial | One-sentence viewer takeaway | Pretty footage with no clear relation to the claim |
| Trust | Source card and visual category | Generated footage appears to be original measured testing |
| Economics | Total attempts and accepted seconds | Only best take's price reported; rejected output costs hidden |

Scoring can organize an internal critique but cannot validate audience demand. Keep reasons, not a fake objective beauty score. Owner judgment remains the creative checkpoint; internal QA comes before it.

## Full-episode economics are a separate question

A subscription covering this test does not establish a five-minute episode budget. Estimate accepted generated seconds, average attempts, minimum clip lengths, dialogue rework, and editing labor separately. A good short character shot can be reused sparingly as a recurring setup, but looping it under narration for minutes would reproduce the failed experience. Long stretches of the final episode should earn their screen time with actual evidence and purposeful editing, not be filled just because generation is available.
