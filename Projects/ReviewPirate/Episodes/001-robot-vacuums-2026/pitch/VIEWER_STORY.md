# The viewer's story — script-led revision

This treatment supersedes the house-first narration and the idea that a final checklist can establish film quality. It is a proposed editorial cut, not recorded timing, tested audience response or a finished market ranking. Read it aloud and watch a timed animatic before locking shots. Existing source locators remain in research/EDITORIAL_RESEARCH.md and VIEWER_COMPANION.md.

## The central dramatic question

**Can we turn other people's winners into a choice that makes sense for your home?**

The otter wants to finish choosing. The viewer wants to avoid researching everything and still understand the catch. Our investigation frustrates the easy shortcut, then supplies a better one. These are the same story, not four competing plots.

Credibility accumulates through useful, checkable distinctions. Beauty accumulates through a coherent place, changing composition and purposeful action. Neither needs its own scheduled segment. A shot can carry several functions; it should have one clear thing the viewer is meant to notice.

## Working spoken script

### 1. A real contradiction, immediately

“This vacuum wins overall—and scores two out of six for avoiding obstacles. In the same review.

We compared published reviews to find out what their recommendations actually mean for your home. We didn't test these robots ourselves.

Here's the useful shortcut: choose the job first. Then check what could rule a robot out. Then ask how much work its automation really saves you.”

### 2. Open the award

“Take the Curv 2 Flow. In The Hook Up's April seven-model comparison, carpet vacuuming and hard-floor mopping count double. Object avoidance scores two out of six.

That isn't a contradiction. A strong overall score can hide a weakness that matters more to you.

If cables and toys regularly stay out, I'd put this one aside pending stronger evidence on obstacles. If you keep the floor clear, keep comparing it. Same review. Different buying decision.

And winning that comparison doesn't settle every cleaning question: Vacuum Wars found weak dried-stain cleaning. So we keep that caveat attached to the product.”

### 3. An explanation has limits

“But sometimes opening the review doesn't explain the disagreement.

For the Saros 10R, Vacuum Wars reports about ninety-two percent pickup in its carpet-hair test. RTINGS describes disappointing carpet-hair pickup.

We checked the methods. We still can't establish what caused the gap. And a percentage from one test and a description from another aren't numbers we can average.

If embedded hair is your main problem, that uncertainty belongs in the recommendation. We've linked both tests so you can inspect the evidence most relevant to your carpet.”

### 4. Change the buying question

“There's one more question a winner badge can't answer: which chores are you paying to lose?

The Curv manual still calls for dirty-water tank cleaning, filter care and wiping sensors. An elaborate dock doesn't mean no maintenance.

For basic vacuuming, a simpler Q7 M5 is worth comparing if you'll prepare the floor and empty its bin. For premium convenience on mostly hard floors, the Saros 10R is a starting point from the reviews we checked. Neither is our universal winner.”

### 5. Give the viewer a usable exit

“So our shortlist has conditions attached. Budget vacuuming: compare the Q7 M5. Premium hard-floor convenience: compare the Saros. Embedded carpet hair: the evidence here doesn't justify one confident winner.

The exact versions, dated prices and original reviews are below. You don't need everybody's best robot. You need to know which compromises you're accepting.”

## How this unfolds, rather than how it scores

Times are editing targets, not measured voice duration. The spoken draft is approximately three minutes at a conversational pace; breathe and cut after auditioning, rather than accelerating to hit timestamps.

### Opening, roughly 0–8 seconds

**The viewer arrives expecting the matrix.** Start with the actual thumbnail's geometry once recovered, already in motion: isolate the Curv column and one attributed result. In the first seconds, show “Overall winner” beside “Object avoidance 2/6,” with The Hook Up credited. This is a concrete reason to listen, not a claim that we are authoritative.

Reveal the approved room and otter in the same opening movement or a clean cut by roughly second three. Keep the evidence insert anchored while revealing the setting. The otter has almost placed the product card into a small chosen pile; the caveat interrupts his hand. He has a goal and a small setback immediately. No scenic establishing shot before the argument, no long frozen chart.

**What changes:** “Another ranking” becomes “there is something inside these rankings worth understanding.” The visual promise and factual hook arrive together. We have earned curiosity, not yet blanket trust.

### Orientation, roughly 8–27 seconds

The scope disclosure accompanies the larger source map; it doesn't arrive as an apologetic closing disclaimer. Then the crowded map reorganizes into three buyer questions: job, veto, remaining work. The otter stops collecting winner badges and begins sorting. Show the recommendation path briefly; don't force eight tiny names to be read at once.

**What changes:** the viewer knows what we did, what we did not do and where the story is going. The open question is now “what would rule one out for me?” Relief begins before any final verdict.

### First answer, roughly 27–70 seconds

Enlarge the real weighting rule and source score, then let the otter's gaze motivate the low camera move to the cable. The room shot makes a preference tangible; it doesn't simulate a Curv failure. His hand considers lifting the cable. The small irritation is recurring preparation, not a household disaster.

Return to the same product card with the obstacle caveat attached. It moves into a buyer-specific set-aside lane, not a universal trash pile. Briefly attach the contrary stain finding: this prevents the audience treating our explanation of one award as endorsement of every claimed strength.

**What changes:** the viewer has learned one mechanism behind an award, applied it to a home, and seen a decision change. Credibility grows from fair explanation plus contrary evidence. The cinematic action has a consequence on the board.

### Complication, roughly 70–110 seconds

The next card initially seems easier: two sources, same model, same broad job. Place the Saros name consistently; show 92% with its test label and the separate qualitative finding. Do not create a fake common axis. A short method insert shows only the fields we actually verified; missing historical details remain visibly unanswered.

The otter starts aligning the cards, stops, and leaves them apart with a question marker. Cut close to his action and expression; don't stay on an evidence slide for the entire explanation. The rug can supply texture and visual continuity without pretending to explain the unproved cause.

**What changes:** viewers discover we will resist a neat answer when the evidence doesn't support it. The story gets harder instead of repeating the previous operation. They receive a specific action: use the carpet-hair tests, not the overall badge.

### Practical payoff, roughly 110–150 seconds

Move from the rug to the dock area. Use an accurate manual insert and household illustration; don't generate imaginary branded mechanics. The otter sets a small maintenance card beside a price/feature card. A chore has merely changed location, which can provide a quiet comic beat without a scripted gag.

Show basic and premium routes at the same visual scale. The board has become simpler because priorities differ, not because we invented a tournament score. Q7 M5 is explicitly a budget-lane addition, not secretly one of the inherited eight.

**What changes:** the viewer sees what “automation” buys and retains. The choice becomes actionable; this is where charm and useful buying judgment can coexist without another technical lecture.

### Resolution, roughly 150–175 seconds

Show the two bounded starting points and the unresolved carpet-hair path in one legible return to the board. Keep the strongest condition on each card. The otter puts away irrelevant evidence, leaves the useful shortlist, and closes the laptop. He has finished framing his choice, not magically proved a universal champion.

**What changes:** the viewer can leave with a route and a reason. Original-source destinations and chapter links offer deeper viewing without withholding the answer. The emotional resolution is reduced uncertainty and permission to stop researching, with important uncertainty still visible.

## Continuous editorial questions

During a real-time watch, stop wherever the answer changes—not at arbitrary equal intervals:

- What does the viewer currently believe, and did the last shot actually support it?
- What useful question are they waiting for us to answer? Have we paid off the previous one before adding another?
- Can they identify the current model and see what changed in their decision without remembering tiny labels?
- Is our confidence earned by the evidence shown so far? Are attribution, test scope and our interpretation distinguishable?
- Is the image rewarding attention now through action, expression, scale, light or a revealing graphic? A pretty opening does not subsidize a dull middle.
- Is the otter doing something that expresses the decision, or have we reduced him to wallpaper?

These are questions for editing the actual sequence, not numerical ratings or extra on-screen information. A moment may deliberately slow down or leave something unresolved. Repair a lapse by changing the beat, not by piling graphics onto it.

## Critique of this draft

The opening now proves something specific before explaining the method. However, it spends its first substantial case on a model that is not one of the closing starting points. The bridge must make the lesson's relevance clear; if a listening test feels like bait-and-switch, use Curv as a shorter cold open and introduce the eventual buyer lanes sooner.

The middle is still information-dense. Do not solve that by inserting unrelated house footage. Cut repeated qualifications from spoken copy when the same necessary distinction can be made clearly on screen, while retaining audible uncertainty in the hair case.

The otter's small decision story is more coherent than an unrelated domestic emergency, but acting could still become repetitive. Test a reach interrupted, a gaze to a real household condition, and a deliberate separation of cards. If three similar desk shots result, rewrite the blocking before generating more shots.

The full matrix is useful for recognition and breadth; it is not a readable television spreadsheet. Zoom to one relationship immediately, and restore the full map only at meaningful chapter transitions. The current 8×8 audit is coverage, not eight winners. The final recommendation ledger and actual thumbnail recovery remain necessary before the opening can be locked.

This is a bounded pilot script demonstrating synthesis with checked cases. It does not yet fulfill the broader ambition of a complete current-market review. Do not dress that research gap as cinematic confidence.
