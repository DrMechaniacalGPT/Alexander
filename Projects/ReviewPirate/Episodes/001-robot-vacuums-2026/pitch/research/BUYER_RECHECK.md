## Does the video overdo caveats?

The 411-word draft had a real weakness: the narrative delivered an answer, then repeatedly withdrew it. The user should receive decisions, not our research anxiety. Revision three changes this:

1. Open with two named, bounded starting points in fewer than50 words.
2. Organize around **what is on your floor, what can stop the robot, and what work the owner will accept**. These are questions a viewer can answer immediately.
3. Use one unresolved example to demonstrate why a household-specific veto matters. Do not narrate every conflicting model uncovered by research.
4. Put secondary conflict details, dates and comparison candidates in the source companion. This retains honesty while avoiding a procession of disclaimers.
5. End with the exact shopping actions: check the relevant original test, confirm the selected variant, and review current price/return terms. No fake universal winner needed.

Narwal stays in the companion research as a promising but contested comparison lead. Its omission from the short opener now rests on mixed cleaning evidence and limited time, not alleged unavailability. It should not occupy the limited first25seconds. Q7 M5 remains useful because the tradeoff is straightforward: basic scheduled pickup with more manual preparation and maintenance. That is a decision, not a consolation prize.
