# Saros 10R hair evidence: matched-field methods audit

Checked September 26, 2026. **The juxtaposition remains fair as two different published assessments of the same model. It is not a matched experiment or proof either test is wrong. Crucially, “flattened” versus “embedded” does not establish two distinct preparation methods: Vacuum Wars also describes pressing hair into carpet fibers.** Do not illustrate one as loose surface hair and the other as embedded fur.

## What the film can faithfully show

Show two attributed result cards for **Saros 10R — carpet hair**. Vacuum Wars: **92% reported pickup**. RTINGS: **disappointing pickup** (a paraphrase). Put **Different test setups; results are not directly comparable** beneath them. A percentage bar may represent the VW result alone; do not assign RTINGS an invented percentage, use equally scaled bars, or animate an average.

The cinematic bridge may show a generic close-up of hair caught among carpet fibers, labeled illustration. It should visualize the buyer's problem, not recreate either lab's amount, carpet, pressure, passes or observed outcome. Keep the robot prop out of the empirical result moment. Avoid a sequence in which changing hair depth or toggling a setting explains the disagreement: those would turn hypotheses into purported demonstrations.

Suggested precise spoken line:

> Vacuum Wars reports ninety-two percent pickup in its carpet-hair test. RTINGS reports disappointing pickup from carpet. Same model, different tests—and we haven't established what caused the gap.

The existing “flattened-hair” wording is literally supported, but paired with “embedded” it can accidentally imply a proven explanation. The simpler line above is preferable. The companion can retain the exact 2.5-inch descriptor and full provenance table.

## Scientific conclusion and next discriminating step

Both observations can coexist; neither establishes performance in the viewer's home. No legitimate average or inferred effect size follows from these sources. We cannot determine whether settings, hair, carpet, preparation, navigation, firmware, unit variation or measurement practice explains the difference. The correct result is **unresolved transferability**, not “one lab tests loose hair, the other embedded hair.”

The next useful research would be historical test footage/protocol confirmation from each publisher covering load, carpet, settings and run count, followed by a matched rerun if feasible. More unrelated awards or owner anecdotes will not resolve this particular gap. Until then, preserve the household-specific caution and direct viewers to the original evidence.
