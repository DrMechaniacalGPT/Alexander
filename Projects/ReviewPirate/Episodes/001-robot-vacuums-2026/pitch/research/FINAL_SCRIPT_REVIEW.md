# Final independent script stress test

Reviewed the working `NARRATION_DRAFT.txt` and `SCRIPT.md` version 4 against the existing editorial ledger and buyer recheck, September 26, 2026. No new research was necessary for this pass. **Verdict: usable development script with three small editorial corrections; not a market-wide buying verdict.** Do not reopen the whole episode or expand it into a list of every contender.

## 1. The opening is supportable, but the Saros rationale needs one affirmative sentence

The Q7 M5 path is well bounded: basic cleaning, more floor preparation, manual emptying. RTINGS, Vacuum Wars' limited pairwise comparison and Tom's Guide support that practical role. It does not establish Q7 M5 as the cheapest or best available budget robot. Change “basic cleaning” to **“basic vacuuming”** in the opening and closing, because the current wording can be heard as endorsing its limited mop.

The Saros path is defensible as an editorial starting point from RTINGS' overall recommendation and the reviewed navigation evidence, while keeping the carpet-hair caveat. However, the spoken script currently recommends it and then tells the viewer only why they might not want it. That is a persuasion gap, even if the claim map is sound. Add one short positive rationale, preferably by replacing the existing “Take the Saros 10R” sentence:

> The Saros earns its place for navigation and everyday convenience. Carpet hair is where the evidence gets harder to read.

This is an editorial synthesis, not a new measured superiority claim. Navigation remains conditional: RTINGS found cords troublesome in Balanced mode and more cautious settings can leave areas uncleaned. Avoid “excellent at every obstacle,” “best mop,” or “reliable for years.” The owner diagnostic cannot establish those.

The most useful alternative opener, if tighter scope is desired:

> For premium convenience on mostly hard floors, compare the Saros 10R. For budget vacuuming, look at the Q7 M5—if you'll clear clutter and empty its bin. These are starting points from the reviews we checked. Embedded pet hair needs a closer look.

This is 42 words by whitespace counting and makes the limited evidence scope audible without turning the opener into a disclaimer. Existing opening is also acceptable if its on-screen synthesis disclosure is legible; the bounded alternative is preferable if the title implies the entire market has been surveyed.

## 2. All three named products are Roborock: real selection risk, not automatic factual error

The two recommendations and the negative example all concern one brand. That does not invalidate them, but a viewer can reasonably infer a Roborock comparison or a brand-sponsored piece rather than a synthesis of robot-vacuum reviews. The research includes other brands, yet the film never demonstrates that breadth. Do not solve this by adding a token competitor or falsely calling the sample representative.

The strongest omitted competitor to the hard-floor path is **Narwal Flow 2**. Vacuum Wars' three-month mostly-hard-floor household trial with a shedding dog is directly relevant; The Hook Up also provides a useful positive hair result. Contrary Tom's Guide results—weak large-debris pickup and repeat-pass qualifications—keep it from becoming an effortless substitute. The live manufacturer page observation means availability is not a defensible reason to omit it.

Recommended minimal remedy: in the companion, visibly pair the premium starting point with **“Also compare: Narwal Flow 2 — useful hard-floor/home evidence, but conflicting pickup results and app/consumable tradeoffs.”** Link both positive and contrary reviews. Add an on-screen source-companion cue during the premium recommendation. No extra spoken model name is required for the pilot if the title and opener promise starting points rather than a definitive “best of 2026” ranking.

If the final public title remains “The Best Robot Vacuum for Your House,” the stronger remedy is one spoken line near the close: **“For a mopping-focused home, also compare the Narwal Flow 2; its reviews disagree on pickup.”** That is a comparison lead, not a third recommended winner. It costs about 25 words and should replace repetition, not extend the runtime. My preference is the scoped opener and visible companion competitor, preserving the current film's focus.

The budget omission is different: the available second source primarily compares Q7 M5 against another Roborock, Q7 L5. Do not imply that this establishes a cross-brand budget winner. Current “starting point” language already handles that limitation adequately.

## Recommended edit set

1. Replace opening with the 42-word scoped version, or at minimum change “basic cleaning” to “basic vacuuming.”
2. Give Saros one positive rationale before the hair disagreement; replace an existing transition rather than adding another research caveat.
3. Say “April seven-model comparison” and “scores two out of six.”
4. Make Narwal's positive and contrary evidence visible in the premium comparison companion. Do not claim its omission reflects lack of stock.
5. Leave the owner anecdotes and expanded maintenance details out of narration. They have already improved the buying checklist; more facts would weaken this short story.

No additional substantive research is required to review this pilot's artistic direction. Before publishing purchasing advice, retain the existing release gate for current offers, exact variants, source footage/timecodes and pronunciation. The script should be judged as a concise, accountable review synthesis, not sold as completion of the broader market audit.
