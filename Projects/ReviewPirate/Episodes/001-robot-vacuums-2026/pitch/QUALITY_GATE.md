# Working standard after the rejected sample

This is an operational correction, not a postmortem ceremony. The prior sample remains rejected. Its renderer, local voice and Field Notes treatment are not the default for the next attempt.

## Separate three kinds of success

1. **Research success:** a claim is supported, qualified, or removed. The source and product identities are exact. A missing result is not a negative test. New counterevidence is allowed to change the recommendation.
2. **Creative success:** the words sound natural and the images, movement and sound hold attention while communicating something useful. A free tool, reproducible script or completed render provides no evidence of this.
3. **Production success:** accepted creative choices can be made consistently at known cost and effort. A cherry-picked vendor showcase or one lucky generation does not establish this.

All three matter. A passed technical check cannot compensate for a failed creative gate.

## Before spending or rendering

- Inspect the creative references and state exactly which aspect each demonstrates. Distinguish a full film from a model demo, and viewed motion from text-only documentation.
- Recover prior owner-approved assets or explicitly record that they are missing. Do not quietly replace the visual starting point.
- Write the useful opening and what every shot contributes before selecting rendering infrastructure.
- Produce a few serious still references to establish the look. They are not a video sample and should not be presented as one.
- Name the highest-risk feature to test first. Here it is appealing character/voice performance, not file export.
- Confirm account access and actual output cost, including duration minimums and output count. Request a purchase only after the specific test is defined.

## Before owner delivery

- Full normal-speed viewing and actual listening, including difficult words and transitions. Record whether this was actually performed and by whom. No waveform or screenshot can substitute for hearing delivery.
- Source check of every displayed/spoken fact, including product variant, test conditions, date and inference boundary. Keep unknowns visible in research, not implied away in copy.
- Aesthetic comparison with the selected reference: material quality, acting, camera, continuity and pacing. Say what still falls short.
- Identify and repair the single weakest shot or sentence. If fixing it cannot meet the intended bar, reduce scope or present the pitch/reference evidence rather than a knowingly poor video.
- Deliver the decision first, then links to deeper work. Do not call the owner a QA department; basic review comes before the creative checkpoint.

## Continued-work loop

After each coherent artifact, ask: what could change the decision; what claim is least secure; what part is least enjoyable; and what independent check can expose an error? Execute the highest-value available check, record what changed, then reassess. Continue independent work when generation is blocked by account access. Avoid unbounded cataloguing or extra documents that do not change the film.

This pass used that loop to uncover real missing coverage, competing mopping results, an incorrect stock extraction, a cheaper current generation route and a more focused opening. Future reports should give equally concrete evidence of revision, not simply state that a review occurred.

## Honest stopping boundary

A completed pitch can be delivered without claiming the episode or motion test is complete. Account authentication, a purchase decision, owner creative selection and final release are distinct dependencies. Preserve exact next actions so a later session can resume without repeating discovery. No media uploads or subscriptions are implied by source-code checkpointing.
