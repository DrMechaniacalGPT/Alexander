we watch the reviews
so you do not have to watch all the reviews

## the business

Review Pirate is a YouTube-first review synthesis business.

the raw material is everything already published about a product

- expert reviews
- laboratory testing
- YouTube reviews
- long-term owner reports
- forums
- support complaints
- manufacturer claims
- retailer reviews
- firmware changes
- pricing

the product is an original research synthesis
turned into a video people can discover
when they are trying to decide what to buy

not

another person unboxing one unit
and declaring it amazing after forty minutes

## the promise

for a useful episode

watch
read
and reconcile
enough of the review ecosystem
that a viewer learns something
they would not get from any one source

show

- what reviewers broadly agree on
- what they disagree on
- why they disagree
- which sources actually tested the claim
- which claims are repeated marketing
- which problems appear only after months of ownership
- what kind of buyer each conclusion applies to

the aggregation is the value

the video is distribution

## the audience

people close to a purchase

that matters

a million bored viewers
can be worth less than
fifty thousand people
currently deciding
where to spend a thousand dollars

## channel position

the recurring idea

**we read and watched everything**

possible formats

### the consensus

"I watched 30 reviews of X. Here is what they actually agree on."

### reviewer civil war

"Seven experts picked five different winners. Here is why."

### after the hype

"Six months later: what owners discovered that launch reviews missed."

### X versus Y

not a spec sheet duel

map the real areas
where independent reviewers disagree
and identify the hidden variable

### best of a category

compare multiple serious review systems
rather than creating one more arbitrary ranking

## discovery

videos have to be found

priority order

1. high-intent search
2. timely launches
3. suggested-video adjacency
4. evergreen category guides
5. Shorts cut from strong long-form findings

titles should match real purchase questions

examples

- Best Robot Vacuum 2026? I Compared the Expert Rankings
- I Watched 30 Reviews of the iPhone 18 Pro
- Sony vs Bose: What Reviewers Actually Disagree About
- The 5 Problems Owners Found After Six Months

do not make titles clever
at the expense of searchable

save the jokes
for after the click

## editorial firewall

affiliate economics can decide
which category is worth researching

they cannot decide
what the research says

a sponsor can buy attention

not the conclusion

## the moat

not AI video

everyone has AI video

the moat is

- better source collection
- better source judgment
- deeper reconciliation of disagreement
- a growing archive of claims and reviewer behavior
- fast production when new products launch
- trust

automation should make
the researcher faster

not make the research disappear
