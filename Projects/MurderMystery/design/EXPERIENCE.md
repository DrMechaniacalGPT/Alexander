## How this changes our proposal

**Agency should be supported, not demanded continuously.** Some guests enjoy structured shared moments. Retain brief public orientation/reveal beats around private choices as a plausible hybrid, rather than equating scripted material with poor design. The first simulator compares private dialogue; it should not dictate the final human format.

**Role intensity can vary without making roles disposable.** AR3 identifies thin participation; AR5 describes successful deliberate casting. Do not fix this with equal secret counts. Give a quieter role consequential responses and inbound reasons for others to approach them, without requiring equal speech volume or aggressive initiation.

**Write for immediate use.** AR1–2 motivate first-person, directly usable instructions and optional sample openers. Our card layout should reduce translation from “your character should…” into playable action. It still needs reader testing; a clean PDF does not prove clarity.

**Limit simultaneous systems.** Factions, passwords, moral dilemmas, trade and special powers may each be interesting, but their combined cost matters. AR4 supports staging complexity rather than adding every mechanic at once. Combat is outside our first design.

**Solution and experience diverge.** AR1 reports enjoyment despite no correct solver. Keep supported accusations, participation and meaningful decisions separate in reports. We have no evidence here that deliberately frustrating discovery improves enjoyment.

## What these accounts do not establish

They do not validate the causal claim that morally ambiguous choices improve dinner parties, the best number of disclosures, typical rates of improvisation, or what happens after a role reveals every secret. Those remain our design hypotheses. The existing D015 video transcript supplies one example of invented rumors and imperfect recall, not a population estimate.

## Sampling and access

A bounded read-only research agent sought first-person experiences distinct from the prior 15 records, favoring contrary experiences over ratings aggregation. Commercial live shows, cooperative case-file puzzles, promotional replies and pre-event questions were excluded from the coded sample. An RPGnet Curse of the Pharaoh review surfaced excerpts but failed to open twice and was excluded. Individual Reddit comment links failed; the parent page supplied readable comment text, so author identifiers and the parent URL are retained. Query log is in RESEARCH.md. No purchases, interviews or live party observations occurred.
