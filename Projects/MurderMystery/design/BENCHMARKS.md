# Should we simulate an existing free game?

**Yes, as a second test—not as the first implementation dependency, and not as behavioral ground truth.** Start with a tiny original situation whose facts we control. Then an independently authored scenario can challenge assumptions shared by our simulator and our own writing. Defer a complete original game until that representation supports a consequential encounter.

A read-only research agent inspected primary listings, terms and available packets on September 26, 2026. Three complete authored mystery packets were directly inspected; the Way out West packet was not ordered, and Secret Hitler was inspected as an adjacent rules-based control. No third-party game files are copied into this repository. The observations below describe stated terms, not a legal determination about every proposed use.

## Recommended sequence

1. **Original controlled fixture:** four roles, one dilemma, two independent information routes and a few windows. It tests mechanics and makes authored variants easy to inspect. It is not a miniature commercial claim.
2. **External mapping:** map Élysée first for its small cast and competing goals, without copying its text into the MIT repository. Document which mechanics our representation handles and which require bespoke adjudication. Puritania is a later scale/structure comparison.
3. **Use/asset decision before adaptation or publication:** establish the precise intended use and applicable terms. NC/SA materials are not unrestricted commercial content; the repository's root MIT license cannot silently replace their terms. Where permission is unclear, request it later or select an original fixture. No permission request has been sent.
4. **Bounded external runs if appropriate:** freeze the adaptation, log every approximation, and inspect traces. A failed port can reveal missing engine capabilities rather than a defect in the original game.
5. **Original one-scene slice:** extend toward eight roles and eventually a full mystery, informed by both exercises. Do not write three polished acts before finding out whether roles have useful choices.

External games provide different authored structures, not known human outcome distributions. Public solutions may already be familiar to a model. Hidden prompts alone do not remove pretrained familiarity; renaming is only a weak diagnostic. Compare genuinely new counterfactual facts and inspect evidence citations without claiming contamination has been eliminated.

The inspected sample did not yield a clearly unrestricted, commercially redistributable, complete multi-player mystery that obviously fits all our aims. That is a bounded search result, not proof none exists. A CC BY single-player Twine work was excluded as the wrong format; an itch.io free PDF with generic license wording remained an unverified lead. Free availability should reduce comparison cost, not tempt us into treating asset rights or adaptation effort as solved.
