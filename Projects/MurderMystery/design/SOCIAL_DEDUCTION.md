# Teams, bluffing and invented information

Added in response to the owner's Mafia/Werewolf comparison. This is a transfer analysis, not a decision to turn the dinner mystery into a hidden-team elimination game. It uses inspected official rules and designer guidance, not newly observed gameplay. [Sources R11–R14](RESEARCH.md).

## What could transfer

Blood on the Clocktower explicitly supports public/private discussion, hidden teams, bluffing and continued participation after death. Its strategy guide describes selective trust, coalitions and sometimes withholding identity even on the cooperative side. Ultimate Werewolf's official moderator material distinguishes team membership from particular wolf roles. These suggest cooperation can be more interesting than every individual maximizing a personal clue collection.

A proposed dinner-game faction might share a limited aim, such as keeping an institution open, while its members disagree about whom to protect and what conduct is acceptable. Team membership need not establish innocence, shared knowledge or unrestricted communication. A coalition can be known to its members without knowing everyone's private actions. Other alliances can form during play.

Prefer **cross-cutting commitments** over an early design in which every clue and friendship divides into two camps. A three-person group risks becoming a dominant information hub or leaving outsiders irrelevant; every member needs meaningful reasons to interact outside it. Shared interests can coexist with private limits and obligations. A common outcome can be an objective, without requiring a points system or declaring every emotional choice a win/loss calculation.

Do not import repeated eliminations by default. A guest who loses all participation early is poorly served by a three-hour dinner product. Continued participation is the useful design principle; Clocktower's exact voting/role system is not being copied. Likewise, an authored historical mystery should not casually adopt an omniscient host who can invent corrective facts during play.

## Inventiveness is not one trait

| Behavior | Example | Proposed handling |
|---|---|---|
| Harmless portrayal | Inventing a favorite drink or habitual phrase | Allow within setting; not murder evidence |
| Speculation | “Maybe Sofia borrowed the key” | Preserve as a hypothesis with source attribution |
| Deliberate deception | Claiming to have been elsewhere | Permit where the role/game allows; claim does not rewrite history |
| Improvised allegation | Claiming to have seen an event not in the packet | A separate high-noise treatment; classify as unsupported testimony and inspect whether the game can recover |
| Canonical fabrication | Producing a new lab report or altering a clue card | Reject as an engine action unless an explicit game mechanic permits it |
| Out-of-character rule assertion | “The rules say Alice cannot lie” when they do not | Treat as a rules issue, not another in-world fact |

Freeform Games permits lying but also has explicit mechanisms for compelled information. That is one design solution, not evidence we must mandate confessions. Our rules must make the boundary between fictional speech and authoritative artifacts legible to humans and models.

For simulation, keep improvisational latitude separate from willingness to deceive, rule compliance and memory error. Start with a constrained portrayal policy and deliberate lying where motivated. Later compare an exuberant improviser and a noisy unsupported-claim policy. Do not add every axis to every experiment at once. The evaluator may mark provenance problems but must not quietly delete lies from other players' views.

## Proposed tests and open questions

Compare the same small situation with no declared faction, a shared limited objective, and conflicting personal commitments within that coalition. Track who receives information, whom outsiders can approach, and whether one member's absence collapses all progress. Agent communication within a faction still goes through normal visibility rules; no automatic shared memory.

For invented claims, inspect whether recipients seek corroboration, whether a fabricated assertion dominates everything, and whether canonical evidence can still constrain conclusions. Our earlier party-video transcript already contained improvised rumors (D015); it is one edited account, not a prevalence estimate. These official strategy documents describe intended possibilities, not how often typical guests invent things. A later observed-play task should code actual claims, corrections, trust and interruptions before declaring our profile categories realistic.
