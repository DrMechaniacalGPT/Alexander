## Independent review and revisions

Two bounded read-only agents reviewed primary design sources and the architecture. Their findings were integrated by the main researcher, not counted as customer evidence. The strongest changes were: make co-design exploration an explicit objective; preserve consequences when adding redundant routes; distinguish global truth, exposure and attention; avoid an automatic confession switch; test meaningful consequences instead of branch quotas; keep quieter roles viable after their information is disclosed.

The earlier proposal to advance only after beating static graph checks is retired as a universal gate. Use those checks to understand what the simulator adds, while also evaluating whether it helps us write richer, more understandable choices. Likewise, profile settings are not assertions about human personality psychology.

## Risks that remain material

A local model may produce articulate, cooperative characters unlike our eventual guests. Summaries may corrupt clues. Rich cards may become too much homework. Factions may drown out individual agency. An excellent video may attract no hosts. A game may be fun without being reliably solved, or correctly solved without being fun. These are reasons to preserve separate observations and revise scope, not reasons to pretend the uncertainty can be settled by another confident memo.

A final consistency review found two implementation gaps that were corrected before publication: E2 now explicitly uses a hand-authored schedule before the W06 scheduler exists; immutable historical truth is distinct from live attendance, possessions, accepted actions and recorded commitments. The first fixture deliberately limits world-changing mechanics. These are specification checks, not executed engine tests.
