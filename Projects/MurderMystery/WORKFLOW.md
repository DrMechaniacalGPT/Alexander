# How we work together

## Outcome

Develop a useful way to explore social game design with local AI, and use it to create a murder mystery that guests enjoy and hosts can run. Short-term products should produce real learning and eventually revenue; reusable methods serve Alexander's longer-term aims. Neither a full task board nor attractive simulated dialogue establishes those outcomes.

The owner has now approved implementation: start with trivial scenarios and incrementally reach a complete text adaptation of an existing game where feasible. The simulation and small fixtures develop together. Neither eight roles nor six conversation windows is a requirement. Keep mechanical completion, plausible player behavior and human validation distinct. Full commercial game/theme and public launch remain separate decisions.

## Division of labor

- **Alexander / integrating agent:** choose the next evidence-producing action, maintain the brief and task state, combine independent work, challenge favorable findings, and bring a concise decision to the owner.
- **Bounded research agents:** search distinct surfaces or test distinct hypotheses; return source records and limitations. They do not independently choose the product or count overlapping sources twice.
- **Dr. Mechaniacal:** set the values and taste that cannot be inferred from data, review major product decisions, participate in or arrange access to real players when needed, and approve spending/public launch where relevant.

The owner need not answer between ordinary tasks. A review comment steers the running objective; it does not require reassigning every next step.

## Work objects

| Object | Job | When to use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Project README | One-minute current answer and links to evidence | Always; keep it short |
| Plan and task board | Outcome, sequence, acceptance tests, current status | Small team and one coordinating thread |
| Source ledger and task synthesis | Reproducible observations, contradictions, method | Every research task |
| Commit | Preserve an incremental change | Whenever useful |
| Focused PR | Review a coherent evidence slice or product decision | At meaningful checkpoints; work may continue on other independent slices |
| GitHub Issue | Assign or discuss a task across threads/people, or trigger a connected cloud task | Add when the markdown board stops being enough |

Do not create two competing task systems merely to feel organized. When Issues are used, link their IDs from the task board and make the issue the active status record.

## Autonomy and stop conditions

For a work cycle, state the outcome, evidence standard, boundaries, and what would count as blocked. Execute related tasks without stopping after each search, commit, or PR. Continue independent authorized work while a reviewable diff waits. Ask for judgment when evidence supports materially different products, before a large spend or public launch, or when only the owner can supply access to people or an account. Report a blocker with the work already completed and the exact missing input.

Work can continue while the turn is active; do not imply that it continues after a final reply without an explicitly configured mechanism. Report what is complete, proposed, blocked or actually running. A GitHub Issue is a specification; filing it alone does not start an agent.

## Research and build loop

1. Write the method and acceptance test before collecting convenient examples.
2. Delegate separable reading across distinct surfaces; keep writes isolated.
3. Integrate, deduplicate, seek negative cases, and record what the evidence cannot say.
4. Commit raw records and a compact synthesis; update the one-minute brief.
5. Check the project outcome. Continue the next independent task or bring a genuine gate decision to the owner.

Fictional personas and agent simulations are adversarial design tools. Interviews, observed parties, and actual purchases are evidence about humans. Neither can be replaced by a polished AI argument.

## Design principles for this cycle

Treat spoken examples as raw material. Preserve meaningful choices, contextual information, participant differences and inspectable consequences; revise names, trait lists and mechanics when better alternatives emerge. Do not demand calibrated human prediction before creative exploration. Do not mistake that freedom for evidence that simulated frequency predicts real parties.

Evaluate opportunity and continued participation from every role's perspective. Keep fixed facts separate from reported claims, exposure separate from memory, and character commitments separate from the participant's habits. Simpler baselines diagnose the machinery; they are not the sole gate for the creative project.

## Standing completion review — approved September 27

Completion of assigned experiments triggers an integrating review, not a request for the owner to invent the next task. For this cycle, the objective is a simulator that supports useful mystery-design experiments: coherent player conversations, inspectable information flow, meaningful guesses, and comparisons that explain which changes help.

After every experiment or agent delivery:

1. Verify the result against its acceptance test and inspect examples, failures, regressions and assumptions. Agent completion is not independent validation.
2. Compare the result with the current objective. Identify the most consequential unresolved issue or uncertainty, including evidence that weakens the preferred approach.
3. Choose the next small useful action: fix a reproducible defect, run a discriminating comparison, simplify an overbuilt mechanism, or extend a successful result to a fresh case. Assign an owner, evidence location, bounded budget and completion test before execution.
4. Execute ordinary authorized follow-ups without waiting for the owner to suggest them. Review again after the follow-up. Keep earlier failures and avoid optimizing only a known example or scoring a correct guess as proof of sound reasoning.
5. Keep the task board and concise decision memo current. PRs preserve reviewable checkpoints; they do not automatically end the cycle. Notify the owner of meaningful changes rather than each minor subtask.

Continue while the turn is active and useful authorized work remains. Stop or ask only for a real access/environment dependency, a material product/taste choice, new spending without an agreed budget, or diminishing returns that call for a different kind of evidence such as human playtesting. State the reason explicitly. This workflow is not a background scheduler and makes no promise of work after the active turn ends; unattended later work needs an explicitly configured persistent mechanism.

### Current-game objective clarified by the owner

Push the current game and the available simulation approaches as far as useful evidence permits before switching games or broadening features. A completed experiment with an unresolved result and a plausible next check should trigger that check. The immediate outcome is an evidence-backed account of what this game/setup allows and where the current simulation succeeds or fails, sufficient to motivate the next direction. Possible findings include ambiguity in the game under explicitly stated interpretations, incomplete information circulation, or limitations of tested models even with adequate information; these are examples, not a required taxonomy or predetermined conclusion. Multiple causes may coexist. Validate encoded rules against source wording before declaring fundamental ambiguity, and do not infer general model incapacity from a small set of failures. Seek decisive comparisons and state residual uncertainty honestly rather than forcing certainty. New games or simulator changes should follow from this understanding, not substitute for resolving tractable questions about the current game.
