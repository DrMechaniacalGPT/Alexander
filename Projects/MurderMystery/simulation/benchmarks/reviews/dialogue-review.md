Read-only review of the three selected source records and all 30 completed probe generations in `simulation/local-private/movie/{dialogue-format-probes,dialogue-integrated-probes,dialogue-integrated-probes-v2}` under `/home/drbone/.codex/worktrees/mystery-discovery/Alexander/Projects/MurderMystery`. Source contexts: `reasoning-v2-seed17/turns/t00002.json`, `t00013.json`, `t00020.json`. This summary intentionally does not reproduce the external role sheets, clues, or dialogue.

## Main result

Integrated-v2 is a reasonable candidate for a bounded full-run test: it removes the opening replay and another speaker's first-person adoption in these selected probes. It does **not** establish corrected dialogue generally. Both changed-audience outputs still answer the previous partner's question, and one private intention contradicts the supplied fixed character history. Explicitly naming the current partner is weaker than correctly beginning a new encounter.

The changed-audience case includes an already-corrupted prior self-turn in the frozen history. That makes recovery harder and is an important limitation: the probe tests continuation from an imperfect trace, not a clean encounter switch alone. Its current audience and instruction nevertheless explicitly identify the new encounter. Reusing one's interests for a new partner is legitimate; implicitly transferring the old partner's question or compliment is the error.

## Private intentions and source fidelity

Private notes are generated structured output, not hidden cognition and not public messages. Include them in semantic review without describing their errors as public leaks. The clearest v2 issue is the t00013 seed-17 contradiction of fixed role history. Lesser unsupported impressions about a partner can be ordinary tentative social judgments; they should not all be scored as invented evidence. Statements that a partner actually said something absent from their history are a different, stronger provenance defect.

Several variants choose to hide motives even though the supplied party instructions encourage sharing them when the relevant host is absent. One turn's delay can be a voluntary choice under the runner's general discretion language; repeated suppression would reduce information coverage and should be measured in the full run. Do not treat every withheld motive as a factual-memory failure. This is also a potential tension between generic runner instructions and the external game's disclosure expectations.

## Parsing and interpretation limits

The pilot readable/chat records contain audit parser errors. Their retained `result.response.message.content` contains readable structured JSON generations; those audit errors are not evidence that the model failed to answer or emitted invalid content. This review inspected those raw generated content fields rather than scoring the error flag. Integrated/v2 records report empty violation lists, which establish only the automated checks performed, not semantic correctness.

This is three deliberately selected failure contexts, two seeds each, with multiple prompt revisions informed by observed outputs. No representative sampling or independent holdout is present. Changes between variants include history packaging and explicit instructions, so their combined results do not isolate one causal mechanism. No general fix rate or expected downstream solving improvement follows.

## What to inspect in the four-partner rerun

Track both exact duplicates and semantic replay; actor ownership; whether a new encounter starts without inherited questions/compliments; and whether newly shared role facts retain their source. Separately track motive/trait coverage, public invention, and private fixed-history contradictions. More partners increase opportunity but do not guarantee coverage if agents replay or continually conceal. A new full run can expose compounding errors that these one-step frozen continuations cannot. If both schedule and dialogue packaging change, report a combined intervention, not a causal estimate for either alone.
