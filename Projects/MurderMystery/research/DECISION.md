**Conditional proceed to a small validation experiment after the owner discussion; do not commit to full production.** This is a plausible learning project and possible modest revenue source. Desk evidence does not yet justify treating it as a dependable war chest, selecting a proven winning theme, or building a platform.

## Why it might work

The available first-person accounts describe enjoyable nights across several formats, alongside host preparation, reading, clue-flow and participation friction. A reliable small-party experience could provide value beyond generating a story. But these problems vary by group; scripts that bore one host reassure another. Our proposed audience is mixed-comfort adults who want sociability **and** a fair deduction, with low host burden. This audience definition is a hypothesis, not a measured segment.

## Why it might not

Established and free kits already produce happy parties. Mischief's primary FAQ closely matches our proposed guided-mingle format. A new theme, AI personalization or a larger research dossier does not establish superior value. We lack real host observations, buyer interviews, paid acquisition results and a comparative playtest. Discovery has reduced uncertainty about alternatives; it has not validated our product.

## Cheapest useful next evidence — proposed, not executed

After discussing audience, concept, openness and budget with the owner:

1. **Host discovery before production.** Speak with roughly 6–8 actual hosts across novices/repeat hosts and lower/higher acting comfort. Ask about their last real party, preparation, abandoned options and spending before showing concepts. Then compare brief anonymous pitches, including a credible existing/free alternative. This small purposive group gives design evidence, not market-size estimates. Record disinterest and recruitment bias.
2. **Choose or stop.** Continue only if there is a specific unresolved host job and a plausible route to reaching those hosts. If alternatives already solve it acceptably or the pitch relies on hypothetical praise, revise or select another business idea. Theme enthusiasm alone is insufficient.
3. **If authorized, one narrow playable prototype.** Eight core roles, short instructions, explicit clue access/fallbacks, no paid app/video infrastructure. Use independent readers to challenge solvability; later simulation checks only modeled brittleness. First draft is an experiment, not a sellable product.
4. **Observe at least three varied groups, including an unassisted host.** Proposed provisional targets: setup at most 45 minutes excluding food/decor, no designer rescue for critical progression, every player able to identify a meaningful action, and private feedback on enjoyment/value. These are decision thresholds to agree, not validated genre standards. Record all failures and actual preparation time. Compare host effort to a credible existing kit where practical; do not attribute improvement without a baseline.
5. **Only after quality evidence, run a bounded paid offer.** Agree spend cap and success rule from observed unit costs. Test actual transactions and support, not merely waitlist interest. Stop if reachable demand cannot support the margin. No outreach, recruitment, purchases or selling has happened in this pass.

## Connection to Alexander

The reusable assets are an inspectable evidence trail, explicit gates, contradiction handling, game-information tests and reproducible unit economics. A shipped product and actual customer behavior would validate that method. A static dinner kit does not test billion-user infrastructure; claiming otherwise would confuse content/product learning with distributed-systems capability. Build a generalized framework only after multiple real projects reveal repeated needs.
