one useful product at a time

the mission needs money
people
trust
and proof that it can build

the cleanest way to get those
is to make things
people choose to use

products are not sermons

they should solve a problem
without requiring the customer
to join the religion

a successful product
can fund the next build

that creates a loop

build
sell
learn
reinvest
repeat

keep projects separate

each should have
its own purpose
leaders
economics
and death conditions

the center should stay small

its job is to

allocate capital
keep shared principles legible
audit conflicts
and help good projects begin

not to run every project

small teams can move

large organizations
accumulate meetings
territory
and reasons to exist

split work
before bureaucracy becomes identity

capital is a tool

take it when the trade is honest
understand what control comes with it
and do not pretend money is morally clean
or morally dirty

the dangerous thing
is dependency

if a project cannot say no
to the person funding it
the price was higher than advertised

attention is also a tool

followers can help
distribution can help
culture can help

but attention without a product
is mostly weather

build

then promote

then see if people stay

a mature project
should ask

can this get cheaper
can this get simpler
can part of it become open
can the margin fall
can the team shrink
can someone else compete easily

success is not
owning a toll booth forever

the mission wins
when useful things become abundant

the first serious build
is Review Pirate
