ideas become real through experiments
not declarations

start with a human problem
something people actually experience
not a theory that merely sounds elegant

find the smallest group
for whom the problem is real
ask what they currently do
what they dislike
what they value
and what they would choose instead

write the hypothesis before building
what do we believe
why do we believe it
what would change our mind

define success in human terms
time saved
money saved
freedom gained
friction removed
joy created
trust improved
waste reduced

attention is not enough
engagement is not enough
growth is not enough
a product can spread
while making life worse

prefer the smallest useful prototype
a spreadsheet before a platform
a script before a company
a neighborhood before a nation
a hundred users before a hundred million

make participation voluntary
make exit easy
do not trap people
with data
contracts
network effects
or manufactured dependency

publish the rules of the experiment
what is being tested
who is affected
what data is collected
how long it runs
how it can be stopped

record predictions
before the outcome is known

afterward publish what happened
including failure
especially failure

look for second order effects
who benefits
who pays
who is excluded
what behavior becomes incentivized
what happens if everyone copies it

try to kill the idea
before scaling it
invite people who disagree
reward the discovery of flaws
a surviving idea is stronger

if the experiment works
repeat it somewhere different
if it only works under one leader
one culture
or one lucky circumstance
it is not yet a system

scale slowly enough
that damage remains reversible
and learning can outrun momentum

the goal is not to prove ourselves right
the goal is to discover
what actually helps people

then build that
