worldcraft

a game for people who think
they know how the world should work

the point is not to win the argument
the point is to discover
what breaks

players

one or more builders
one or more adversaries
one recorder

the builder proposes a world
the adversary tries to destroy it
the recorder tracks assumptions
failures
and surviving principles

round one
the dream

describe the world you want

who has power
how people live
how people work
how people trade
how people disagree
how children grow up
how outsiders enter
how people leave
how rules change
what happens when someone refuses

do not begin with institutions
begin with daily life

what does a good ordinary tuesday feel like

round two
constraints

add reality

limited attention
limited resources
unequal ability
different values
bad luck
crime
corruption
disease
natural disaster
technical failure
migration
aging
scarcity
jealousy
ambition
ignorance

the dream must continue
without assuming these disappear

round three
the villain

give the system
to someone you dislike

they are selfish
clever
patient
popular
and willing to exploit every rule
that is technically allowed

what can they capture
what can they hide
what can they centralize
what can they make irreversible

if the answer is
the right people must stay in charge
the system failed

round four
the saint

give the system
to someone sincere
competent
and convinced they are right

this may be more dangerous

what harm can be done
with good intentions
certainty
urgency
and broad support

round five
the generations

advance twenty five years

the founders are gone
the original crisis is forgotten
the rituals remain
the incentives remain
the bureaucracy remains

what still makes sense
what has become theater
what institutions now exist
only to preserve themselves

advance again

structural integrity
must survive memory loss

round six
the edge cases

test the person
who does not fit

the dissenter
the eccentric
the poor
the rich
the disabled
the genius
the fool
the outsider
the hermit
the fanatic
the person who wants nothing to do with the project

a humane system
must survive people
who do not resemble its designers

round seven
the scale jump

run it for

ten people
a thousand people
a million people
the planet

something that works
for friends
may become coercion
when scaled

something that works
for a nation
may become absurd
for a neighborhood

identify
what must stay local
what benefits from coordination
and what should never be centralized

round eight
the fork

someone disagrees
and wants to leave

what can they take with them

their money
their data
their identity
their work
their relationships
their tools
their reputation

if exit is impossible
participation was not fully voluntary

round nine
the copy

a rival copies the system

they remove the moral language
keep the useful mechanisms
and compete directly

does the idea still work

if success depends
on exclusive ownership
mystique
or loyalty
then the mechanism may be weaker
than the story

round ten
the inversion

take each virtue
and maximize it
until it becomes a vice

transparency becomes surveillance
safety becomes control
freedom becomes abandonment
equality becomes conformity
efficiency becomes fragility
community becomes exclusion
decentralization becomes chaos
certainty becomes dogma
tolerance becomes paralysis

find the point
where more stops being better

scoring

do not use one score

keep a dashboard

freedom
human dignity
voluntary participation
resilience
reversibility
clarity of power
ability to dissent
ability to exit
material usefulness
environmental cost
complexity
capture risk
dependence on exceptional leaders

the disagreement over the scores
is part of the game

winning

a world does not win
by surviving unchanged

it wins
when the builder is willing
to change it
after learning where it breaks

the strongest outcome
is not a perfect world

it is a design
with explicit weaknesses
known tradeoffs
easy exit
limited blast radius
and a method
for becoming less wrong

then play again
